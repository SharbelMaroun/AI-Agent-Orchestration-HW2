In 1925, a relay of sled dog teams covered 674 miles through Alaska blizzards to deliver diphtheria antitoxin to the epidemic-stricken town of Nome. The final leg was driven by a dog named Balto, whose statue now stands in New York's Central Park. In 1957, the Soviet Union launched Laika, the first living being to orbit Earth, in Sputnik 2 — a one-way mission she did not survive but which made every subsequent human spaceflight possible. Hachiko, an Akita in Tokyo, walked to Shibuya Station every afternoon for nine years to meet a master who had died at work — Japan still holds an annual ceremony in his memory. Stubby, a stray terrier mix, served in 17 World War I battles, captured a German spy, and was decorated by three U.S. presidents. Each story is celebrated not because the dogs were unusual specimens but because their breed-wide character — loyalty, persistence, courage — is precisely the trait humans most consistently reward. No comparable cultural canon exists for any other companion species.
