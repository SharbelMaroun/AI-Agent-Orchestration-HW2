Dog owners walk an average of 23 to 30 minutes more per day than non-owners, accumulating roughly 200 additional minutes of physical activity per week. This exceeds the World Health Organization's recommended weekly threshold of 150 minutes of moderate exercise for adult cardiovascular health. The activity is consistent: dog walks happen rain or shine, in winter and summer, on busy weeks and quiet ones, because the dog's needs do not pause. Self-reported gym memberships, by contrast, show attendance declines averaging 60% within six months of signup. A 2013 study in the Journal of Physical Activity and Health found dog owners were 57% more likely to meet weekly activity guidelines than non-owners, even after controlling for age, income, and pre-existing health conditions. For an aging adult, replacing a daily structured walk with a brisk outdoor dog walk produces measurable improvements in cardiovascular endurance, balance, and bone density within twelve weeks. The dog provides what willpower alone cannot reliably supply: external accountability.
