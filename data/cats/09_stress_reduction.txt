---
source: Adamle et al., University of Minnesota Stroke Institute, 2008
type: study
relevance: stress, cardiovascular, health
---

A ten-year University of Minnesota Stroke Institute study tracking 4,435 adults found that cat owners had a 30 to 40 percent lower risk of death from cardiovascular disease than non-owners. The lead researcher, Adnan Qureshi, called the finding "more or less an unintentional surprise" — the study was originally designed to examine other variables. He hypothesized that the effect operated through stress reduction rather than physical activity, since cat ownership does not produce the increased exercise that drives the dog-mortality literature. Petting a cat measurably lowers cortisol within sixty seconds. The vibration frequency of a domestic cat's purr — between 25 and 150 hertz — corresponds to the range used in therapeutic ultrasound for healing bone and soft tissue. Studies have correlated cat ownership with lower resting heart rates and reduced anxiety in clinical populations. The protective effect appears to operate quietly, without the prescriptive lifestyle change a dog requires. The cat heals you simply by being a cat in your house. You do not have to do anything except sit still.
