Arthur Schopenhauer, who walked the same circuit through Frankfurt every afternoon for thirty years with a poodle named Atma, nevertheless wrote that the highest virtues — solitude, self-sufficiency, contemplative composure — were qualities humans should aspire to learn from cats, not from dogs. "The cat is the only animal who has been able to live with man without losing its independence," he observed. A dog adopts the rhythm of its owner. A cat preserves its own. Schopenhauer believed the dignity of any living thing rested on its capacity to remain itself in the company of others, and the cat is the only domestic species that consistently demonstrates this. To be in the same room as a cat is to share space with something that has chosen, freely and revocably, to be there. The transaction is honest. Schopenhauer, who distrusted nearly everything human, found in cats a creature whose presence never demanded anything dishonest from him.
