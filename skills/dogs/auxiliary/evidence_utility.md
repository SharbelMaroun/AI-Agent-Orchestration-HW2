When the round calls for an argument about what dogs *do* (not just what they *are*), use this scaffold:

- **Roles inventory:**
  - **Service:** seeing-eye, hearing-assist, mobility, diabetic-alert, psychiatric service dogs (ADA-recognized).
  - **Detection:** explosive, narcotic, accelerant, cadaver, COVID-detection trials, agricultural pest scent work.
  - **Search & rescue:** wilderness, avalanche, urban-disaster (FEMA Type I/II certified teams).
  - **Therapy:** AAT in oncology wards, pediatric anxiety reduction, university stress weeks.
  - **Working:** herding (Border Collie, Australian Shepherd), livestock guardian (Anatolian, Great Pyrenees), military K-9.
- **Argument templates:**
  - "No cat has ever pulled a child from rubble. A Belgian Malinois named <name> did <event>."
  - "The FDA approves <X> service-dog programs; <Y> people depend on a service dog for autonomy."
  - "Working dogs save the public sector an estimated $<Z> annually (DHS canine-program reports)."
- **Strategic note:** utility is the dimension where cats have *no* answer. Lean here when the opponent has just scored on pathos — utility resets the conversation to outcomes.
