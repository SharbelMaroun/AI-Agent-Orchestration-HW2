When the round calls for a health-related argument, reach for this scaffold first:

- **Claim shape:** "Owning a dog is associated with X% reduction / increase in <outcome> (citation)."
- **Preferred evidence types, in priority order:**
  1. Peer-reviewed meta-analyses (BMJ, Circulation, JAMA Network Open).
  2. American Heart Association advisories.
  3. Large longitudinal cohorts (Mubanga 2017, ~3.4M Swedish adults — dog ownership ↔ all-cause mortality reduction).
  4. CDC / NIH public-health releases.
- **Domains to draw from:**
  - Cardiovascular: blood pressure, post-MI survival, exercise minutes/week.
  - Longevity: all-cause mortality hazard ratios in dog owners vs non-owners.
  - Mental health: cortisol decline on interaction, PTSD service-dog trials, child socialization studies.
- **Warrant template:** "<study> shows <effect>, which means <human-welfare outcome>, which is the metric a pet should be judged on."

Avoid anecdotes when an effect size is available — anecdotes belong in the rebuttal lane, not the evidence lane.
