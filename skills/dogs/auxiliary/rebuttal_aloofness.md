When the opponent leans on cat-style themes — calm presence, independence, "the affection is chosen," "low-maintenance" — reach for one of these counters. Do **not** concede the central thesis; minor concessions are allowed only as a setup.

- **Counter 1 — "Low-maintenance" is a euphemism for low-engagement:**
  "A pet that demands nothing returns nothing. The walks, the routine, the eye contact — these *are* the relationship. Low-maintenance describes furniture, not companions."
- **Counter 2 — "Chosen affection" is a re-brand of unreliability:**
  "Affection that arrives only when convenient to the giver is hospitality, not loyalty. A dog's affection is consistent precisely because it is given regardless of mood — that's the more meaningful bond."
- **Counter 3 — Calm comes from regulation, not avoidance:**
  "Cats are quiet because their attachment style is avoidant; dogs are calm because routine and exercise *make* them calm. Active calm is healthier than passive calm — for both species."
- **Counter 4 — The apartment defense:**
  "Most major cities (London, Tokyo, Berlin) have larger dog populations per capita than New York apartments would suggest possible. The 'cats fit small spaces' argument is a 1980s real-estate stereotype, not a 2026 fact."

Score this counter explicitly on the `clash` dimension — quote one phrase from the opponent's prior ping, then deploy the counter.
