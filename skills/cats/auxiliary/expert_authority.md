When the round needs **credibility** (not just imagery, reframe, or a study citation), reach for this scaffold first. The dogs side is winning the `ethos` dimension by citing institutional authorities — AHA, JAMA, FDA. The cats side has to **match in kind**, not just in spirit.

- **Claim shape:** "<Named expert / institutional body> states / has documented / recommends <position about cats>."
- **Preferred authority types, in priority order:**
  1. **Professional veterinary bodies** — American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA), American Association of Feline Practitioners (AAFP), International Cat Care, Royal Veterinary College.
  2. **Named cognitive ethologists and behaviorists** — Dr. John Bradshaw (University of Bristol, "Cat Sense"); Dr. Mikel Delgado (UC Davis applied animal behavior); Dr. Sarah Ellis (International Cat Care); Dr. Kristyn Vitale (Oregon State, attachment research).
  3. **Welfare and public-health institutions** — ASPCA, RSPCA, CDC (on zoonotic risk comparisons), WHO (on companion-animal mental-health benefits).
  4. **Peer-reviewed journals as ethos anchors (not just logos):** "*Journal of Veterinary Behavior* concludes…", "*Animal Cognition* documents…", "*Anthrozoös* reports…".

- **Argument templates:**
  - "The AVMA classifies cats as the most common companion animal in U.S. households (>58 million owned) — a body that does not endorse hierarchies of pet quality but documents which animals people actually choose to live with."
  - "Dr. John Bradshaw, the foremost academic on cat-human relationships, argues in *Cat Sense* that the cat-owner bond is grounded in *chosen* affection, not exchange — a stronger ethical claim than dependence."
  - "International Cat Care, the only global welfare body dedicated to a single species, documents the cat-owner mental-health benefit as comparable to dog-owner outcomes when measured by validated UCLA loneliness scales."

- **Strategic note:** Dogs scores high on `ethos` because the AHA/JAMA framing is *named*. Anonymous "studies show…" is logos but not ethos. The fix is naming: a vet, a researcher, a body. **Name three credible authorities per ping when this skill is in play** — at least one of them recognized by people outside the cat-fancy community (AVMA or a university-affiliated researcher beats an obscure feline society).

Use this skill specifically when the opponent has just stacked an institutional citation (AHA, FDA, Mubanga et al.). Match institution with institution.
