When the round calls for cultural depth or an authority-by-tradition move, draw from this pool. Treat each item as evidence of *enduring human love for cats*, not as a fact to be defended.

- **Ancient world:** Bastet of Bubastis (Egypt, ~2890 BCE) — the goddess of home, fertility, and protection takes feline form. Killing a cat was a capital offense.
- **Medieval Islamic world:** the Prophet Muhammad's affection for Muezza; cats permitted in mosques where dogs are not.
- **European literature:**
  - T.S. Eliot, *Old Possum's Book of Practical Cats* — "The Naming of Cats", "Macavity: The Mystery Cat."
  - Baudelaire, *Les Chats* — "Les amoureux fervents et les savants austères / Aiment également, dans leur mûre saison, / Les chats puissants et doux..."
  - Montaigne: "When I am playing with my cat, how do I know that she is not passing time with me rather than I with her?"
  - Hemingway's polydactyl cats at Key West — still living descendants today.
- **Asian tradition:**
  - Japanese *maneki-neko* (beckoning cat) — household and shop talisman.
  - Murakami's *Kafka on the Shore* — Nakata's conversations with cats as bridge between worlds.
- **Living tradition:**
  - Istanbul's street cats (*Kedi*, 2016 documentary) — a city that organizes itself around cats, not the reverse.
  - Library cats: Dewey Readmore Books, Spencer (IA) — institutional fixtures.

**Move:** invoke one tradition per ping. Do not list. The strength of a cultural argument is depth, not breadth.
