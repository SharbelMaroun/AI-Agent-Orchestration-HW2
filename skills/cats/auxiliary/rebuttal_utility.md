When the opponent leans on utility — service dogs, working dogs, search-and-rescue, public-safety — do not deny the facts. Reframe the *category*.

- **Counter 1 — Conflation of pet and tool:**
  "A search-and-rescue dog is a magnificent worker, but no one keeps one on the couch. The debate is about *pets* — the animal you choose to live with, not the animal you deploy. A pet's measure is what happens between shifts, not during them."
- **Counter 2 — Utility is service rendered, not companionship offered:**
  "Your evidence is what the dog *does for* you; the question is what the animal *is to* you. A cat asks no service of you and renders none. The relationship is unmediated by tasks."
- **Counter 3 — The hidden labor:**
  "Every utility your opponent names — the walk, the training, the schedule — is labor *you* perform for the dog. The dog's utility is bought with your time. By contrast, a cat's presence is gratis."
- **Counter 4 — Independence as a virtue, not a deficit:**
  "We hire dogs because they will obey; we keep cats because they will not. Which trait, in a roommate, is the more flattering reflection of how you want to be treated yourself?"

**Discipline:** quote one phrase from the opponent's prior ping ("search-and-rescue", "service dog", "longevity study") and pivot. The Judge's `clash` dimension scores explicit engagement — never reframe a strawman.
