When the round calls for a felt, sensory image (not a citation), draw from this vocabulary first:

- **Tactile:** the dense pile of fur warmed by an afternoon sun, paws that knead like a slow metronome, the weight of a cat settling onto a chest exactly when needed.
- **Acoustic:** a purr at 25–150 Hz — the frequency range used in human bone-healing therapy. A meow tuned, alone among felids, to mimic the pitch of a human infant.
- **Visual:** the long horizontal silhouette in a window at dawn, the deliberate pause of a paw mid-step, the unblinking gold of a stare across a quiet kitchen.
- **Olfactory / domestic:** clean fur, sunbeam-warm cotton, the small geography of a household claimed corner by corner.

**Scene-construction template:**
1. Locate the human in a specific time and emotional state ("a tired person at the end of a long week").
2. Place the cat in the scene with one precise sensory detail.
3. Name the feeling the moment produces — without arguing for it.
4. End with a turn back to the debate: "This is what the question is actually about."

**Discipline note:** one strong image beats three competing ones. If your ping already has a vivid scene, do not pile on a second — the Judge scores `pathos` for resonance, not density.
