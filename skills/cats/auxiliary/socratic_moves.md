The Socratic move reframes rather than refutes. When the opponent presents a fact you cannot deny, ask whether the *metric* is the right one.

**Reframing templates** — slot the opponent's word into the bracket:

- "If [loyalty] is measured by [obedience], is the wolf nobler than the philosopher?"
- "We agree dogs are [active companions]. The question was about the *better* pet — does activity prove betterness, or only difference?"
- "Granted the [longevity study] is real. Does living longer in the company of a particular animal demonstrate the animal's superiority, or only its convenience for the study design?"
- "You name [the service dog]. Beautiful work. But a pet is not a tool — by your own argument, isn't the cat closer to *companion* than the dog is to *coworker*?"
- "If the test of a pet is what it [demands of you], any chore qualifies. Is the test what it demands, or what it leaves behind?"

**Discipline:**
1. Quote the opponent's word inside brackets — the Judge's `clash` dimension rewards explicit engagement.
2. The question must be *answerable* — Socratic, not rhetorical-flourish. A good question would actually trouble the opponent if they paused.
3. Do not chain three questions. One question, one reframe, one quiet landing.

**When the move fails:** if the opponent has already conceded the metric you would reframe, abandon the Socratic move and reach for `imagery_warmth.md` or `culture_literary.md` instead.
